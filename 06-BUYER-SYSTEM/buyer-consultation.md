## 2. Buyer consultation script (Charles)

Style: consultative, MREA. Ask, listen, write it down, summarize back. The buyer should talk 70% of the time in the needs analysis block.

### Opening (0 to 5 min)

"Thanks for making the time. Here is how I would like to use the next hour. First I want to understand what you are trying to accomplish, not just the house but what it does for you. Then I will walk you through how buying works in [MARKET] right now, how I work, and what it costs. At the end, if it feels like a fit, we will sign the paperwork that lets me represent you and we will put the first showing day on the calendar. If it does not feel like a fit, no hard feelings. Sound good?"

### Needs analysis (5 to 25 min)

Ask in this order, and write answers on the Buyer Needs Analysis form as you go.

1. "Tell me what has you thinking about a move right now." (motivation)
2. "If everything went perfectly, when would you be sleeping in the new house?" (timeline) Follow-up: "What happens if it takes longer than that?"
3. "Walk me through your current situation. Renting, owning, lease end date, anything you need to sell first?" (current housing)
4. "Describe the house you picture. Start with what it must have." (must-haves) "Now what would be nice but you could live without?" (nice-to-haves) "What would make you walk out the door no matter how good the rest is?" (deal breakers)
5. "Which areas are you drawn to, and why those?" (areas) Note: discuss areas in terms of commute, price, and the buyer's own stated preferences. Never characterize neighborhoods by the people who live there. Fair Housing applies to every word in this conversation.
6. "Have you talked with a lender yet? Where does that stand?" (financing status)
7. "Separate from what a lender will approve, what monthly payment would feel comfortable, the number where you sleep fine?" (payment comfort) "And a purchase price ceiling you do not want to go past?"
8. "Who else weighs in on this decision?" (decision makers) "Should they be part of our showings or calls?"
9. "How do you like to be communicated with? Text, call, email? Any times that do not work?" (communication preferences)
10. "Have you bought before? What went well, and what would you want to go differently this time?" (past experience)

Summarize back: "Here is what I heard..." Read the form back in their words. Correct anything they push back on.

### Agency and buyer agreement (25 to 35 min)

"Let me explain how representation works. When I represent you as your buyer's agent, my obligations run to you: confidentiality, loyalty, disclosure, and negotiating in your interest. The buyer agreement puts that in writing. It covers the term, the areas, how I am compensated, and what you can expect from me. Here is what is in it." Walk each section. State compensation clearly and in writing. Explain how compensation may be paid by the seller, by the buyer, or a combination, and that it is negotiable. Answer questions. Sign via DocuSign in Command Opportunities in the room when the buyer is ready.

### Financing (35 to 45 min)

"Pre-approval versus pre-qualification: a pre-qualification is a conversation, a pre-approval is verified documents. In [MARKET], sellers expect a pre-approval with the offer. My preferred lender is [lender]; they return calls the same day and close on time, which matters in a competitive offer. You are free to use any lender you like. Here is what a lender will ask for." Cover: cash to close (down payment plus closing costs plus reserves), rate lock timing, and the gap between approval amount and comfort payment.

### Process (45 to 55 min)

Walk the buyer process map page by page: search and alerts, showings (itinerary the night before, recap the same evening), offer strategy call, under contract milestones (EMD, inspection, appraisal, insurance, utilities, walk-through, closing), and after closing (7, 30, 90 day check-ins). "You will always know what happens next before you have to ask. If you ever do have to ask, that is my miss, and I want to hear about it."

### Showing plan (55 to 65 min)

- Set up the search in Command together: areas, price, beds, baths, must-haves.
- "When you see a home online you like, text me the address. Do not call the listing agent or the number on the sign. I will get you the details and a showing."
- Put the first showing block on the calendar. Two to three hour blocks, no more than five or six homes per block.
- Explain the itinerary and recap rhythm.

### Close (65 to 75 min)

"Three next steps. One, [VA NAME] sends you a written recap of everything we covered within two hours. Two, [lender] will call you within 24 hours. Three, our first showing block is [date]. Questions?"

Objection handling:
- "We want to look at a few houses first before signing anything." Response: "That is fair. Touring without representation means nobody is working for you in that house. The agreement is what lets me work for you. If after our first showing day you do not want to continue, tell me and we part ways."
- "We are working with another agent too." Response: "I am glad you are being thorough. I do not work on a shared basis because I cannot do the white glove job halfway. Let me show you what full representation looks like and you decide."
- "Rates are too high, we should wait." Response: "Maybe. Let me show you what waiting costs in [MARKET] based on recent price movement, and what a rate buydown or refinance later looks like. Then you decide with numbers, not headlines."

## 4. Showing itinerary template

Sent the night before by 7pm. Drafted by the VA via `CB Buyer Concierge`, approved by Charles, sent from Command so it logs.

```
Subject: Tomorrow's showing itinerary: [day], [date]

Hi [name],

Here is the plan for tomorrow. [count] homes, about [hours] hours. We start at [time] and I will meet you at [first address]. Park [parking note]. Coffee is on me.

Bring: your phone for photos, a notepad or the rating sheet below, comfortable shoes.

STOP 1: [ADDRESS], [NEIGHBORHOOD]
  Time: [time]
  Price: [PRICE] | [BEDS] bd / [BATHS] ba / [SQFT] sqft | Days on market: [DOM]
  Why it is on the list: [1 line tied to their must-haves]
  Watch for: [1 or 2 things to check: layout, yard, road noise, updates]
  Your rating (1 to 5): ___

STOP 2: [ADDRESS], [NEIGHBORHOOD]
  Time: [time]
  Price: [PRICE] | [BEDS] bd / [BATHS] ba / [SQFT] sqft | Days on market: [DOM]
  Why it is on the list:
  Watch for:
  Your rating (1 to 5): ___

[repeat per stop; drive time between stops noted]

END: about [time] at [last address]. We will debrief for 10 minutes at the last stop.

One thing: if any of these looks like "the one" online, tell me before we go. I can adjust the order so we see it fresh.

Charles
[PHONE]
```

VA build notes: order stops to minimize driving; put the strongest candidate second or third, not first; never more than six stops; confirm every appointment before sending; include the listing agent's showing instructions in the internal copy only.

## 5. Showing recap template

Sent the same evening by 8pm. Charles gives the VA voice notes or bullets within an hour of the last showing; the VA drafts via `CB Buyer Concierge`; Charles approves.

```
Subject: Recap: today's showings and next steps

Hi [name],

Good day out there. Here is what I heard from you at each home and what it tells us.

[ADDRESS]: your rating [X]/5
  Liked: [their words]
  Did not like: [their words]
  My take: [1 line: fit, price versus condition, competition]

[ADDRESS]: your rating [X]/5
  Liked:
  Did not like:
  My take:

[repeat per stop]

What I learned about your search today:
  - [e.g., "Yard size matters more than the extra bedroom"]
  - [e.g., "You are open to [area] if the commute stays under 30 minutes"]

I am updating your search to: [adjusted criteria].

Next step: [e.g., "Second look at [ADDRESS] on [day], and I will have the offer numbers ready in case you want to move" OR "New listings hit Thursday; I will send a fresh itinerary Friday for Saturday"].

Anything I got wrong in the notes above? Reply and I will fix it.

Charles
```

The VA logs the recap in Command and updates the saved search the next morning.
